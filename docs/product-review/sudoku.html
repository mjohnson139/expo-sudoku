Sudoku

Current functionality, controls and states · redesign inventory

Current purpose: a conventional generated 9×9 Sudoku with fast entry, optional notes and mistake feedback, a timer/score loop and app-wide visual themes.

1. Entry and game structure

Difficulty sheet shown on first entry.

Generated Medium board.

Entry flow

  1. Tap Sudoku on the hub.
  2. Choose Easy, Medium, Hard or Expert.
  3. A generated puzzle opens with score 0 and timer 00:00.
  4. Leaving persists resumable progress; the hub can advertise Continue.

3. Menu and secondary states

Game menu over the live board.

State and feedback

  • The selected difficulty is visible in both the status row and difficulty menu.
  • Score increases through play; elapsed time is part of the session record.
  • Optional mistake feedback can identify incorrect entries immediately.
  • Completion presents a finished-game state and makes a new puzzle the next action.
  • Undo/redo preserve intentional input history; notes and final values are distinct.

Accessibility and platform behavior

  • Header and difficulty actions have explicit accessibility labels.
  • The grid must communicate row/column/box context, givens, selection, notes and values without relying on colour alone.
  • Keyboard size, one-handed reach and small candidate text are important device checks.

4. Product/design discussion prompts

Role in the product: Is Sudoku the familiar acquisition experience, the primary daily game, or simply one peer in a puzzle collection?
Score and time: Which behavior should the product reward—speed, accuracy, streaks, difficulty progression, or calm play?
Theme ownership: An app-wide preference currently lives inside this game. Should global appearance move to a shared settings surface?
Difficulty before play: Should first entry always block on a choice, remember the last level, or provide a daily/recommended puzzle?
Mistake model: Is immediate correctness feedback a learning aid, a setting, or contrary to the intended challenge?
Information density: Score, level and time compete with the grid; decide which are central to the future purpose.
Do not lose in redesign: notes versus final entry, immutable givens, undo/redo semantics, error visibility, board legibility, persisted mid-game state and a clear fresh-puzzle choice.
