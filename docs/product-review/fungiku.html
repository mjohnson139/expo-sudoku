1. Rules and entry

Difficulty and board-size choice.

Main board and assistance toolbar.

Player goal

  • Place exactly one mushroom in every row.
  • Place exactly one mushroom in every column.
  • Place exactly one mushroom in every coloured region.
  • No two mushrooms may touch, including diagonally.

3. Status, economy and feedback

Difficulty/menu layer over a live board.

Persistent/visible state

  • Progress: placed mushrooms out of required total.
  • Wallet: coin count persists separately from the current puzzle.
  • Lives: three mushroom icons; incorrect placements consume one.
  • Puzzle: generated seed, coloured regions, solution and player marks persist for resume.

4. Product/design discussion prompts

Teachability: The rule set is less familiar than Sudoku. How should row/column/region/no-touch rules be introduced and recalled?
Primary interaction: Tap means ✕ while double-tap means mushroom. Is that discoverable and accessible enough, especially with drag-to-mark?
Economy: Do coins create a satisfying long-term loop, or add monetization-like language to a free logic puzzle?
Error philosophy: Three lives and immediate correctness checking make this a guided game rather than paper-puzzle simulation. Is that intentional?
Assistance hierarchy: Rule out, Hint and Reveal overlap. Should they remain three paid levels or become one adaptive help action?
Board scaling: 10×10 must coexist with status and two control rows on a phone. Decide which controls can move off the main play surface.
Do not lose in redesign: coloured-region differentiation, all four rules, unambiguous ✕ versus mushroom state, three-life consequences, wallet transparency, undo/redo, hint anchoring, resume, out-of-lives recovery and solved celebration.
